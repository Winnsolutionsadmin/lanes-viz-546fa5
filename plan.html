05 · the ramp

Twenty-four months, four phases, each with a gate

01
Months 1–3

Prove the process on your own cars

Seed with 6–10 cars you control. No client to disappoint while you find the style. The objective is not revenue — it is a public track record and a repeatable production process.

Gate: ≥80% sell-through and one result visibly above comparable sales.
02
Months 4–9

Buy the only real moat

A specialist automotive photographer and a rigid house style. Buyers identify a top seller's listing from the photograph before reading the title. That recognition is the asset — and it is a hire plus a discipline, not a technology.

Gate: two-thirds of listings sourced from outside your own collection.
03
Months 10–18

Differentiate on evidence

Every competitor sells on taste. Sell on proof: what the car is worth against 267,790 bid events, what it will realistically bring, what reserve to set, when to list. Reframes the pitch from “trust our eye” to “check our math.”

No consignor in this market can make that claim.
04
Months 19–24

Work the list nobody else has

1,781 failed-to-sell cars, $108M in refused bids. Owners who publicly proved they want to sell and published the number they turned down. Returns sell +13.1% above the refused bid, 23 days later.

Not a lowball list — an arrive-early-with-a-better-number list.

the honest part

Four risks, and the one that governs

1

Reputation is the slowest asset to build. The incumbents have a decade. The only real shortcut is entering as someone the market already knows — which is worth more than any tooling.

2

Platform dependency is total. Terms, fees or ranking changes hit revenue directly, and there is no second channel with this audience.

3

Thin margin punishes bad reserve calls. One failed car erases the margin on two successful ones.

4

It is a service business until it is a brand. Revenue scales with headcount until recognition starts doing the selling for you.

The platform hands you the audience for free. What you must build is inventory quality, conversion discipline and a recognisable presentation — and the differentiator nobody else in this market can copy is pricing evidence.

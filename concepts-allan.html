So the question isn't how to be early to a listing. It's: where is there a window that isn't a seven-day auction everyone can see? We found two, plus one thing that isn't a window at all.
concept 01

The cars that didn't sell — and where they go

In four months, 1,743 vehicles failed to meet reserve, carrying $107M in high bids. Every one is a seller who publicly proved they want to sell and publicly proved the exact number the market offered them. Only 51 of them (3%) came back to the platform. 1,692 of them went somewhere the platform never saw.

The honest correction: we assumed these were distressed sellers. They are not — when they come back they get 13.1% more than the bid they turned down, median 23 days later. So this isn't a bargain list. It's something better: a list of proven sellers with a published price expectation, 1,692 of whom nobody is talking to. Does that list have value to you?
concept 02

Predicting what surfaces, instead of reacting to it

The only way to beat a public auction is to not be in one. Inventory doesn't appear randomly — it comes from pipelines with a cadence. 85 sellers account for 20% of all lots. Cars come back to market on a measurable clock.

Straight about the limits: 130 reappearances in a four-month window is a thin base to model on. This concept needs the all-time corpus to be more than a hypothesis — and we'd rather say that than show you a forecast built on 130 events. Is a ranked "likely to surface in 90 days" list worth the build, or is your own network already faster than a model would be?
concept 03 — the one that isn't about speed

Everyone sees the listing. Nobody else sees the history.

If the decision happens in a five-minute window, then everything useful has to already be computed when that window opens. That's not a reflex problem, it's an inventory-of-knowledge problem — and it's the only one here a competitor can't solve by writing a scraper.

You know this market and we have the data on it. Those are different things, and the second is worth nothing without the first. We killed the speed concept ourselves on slide one — we'd rather do that than sell you something that doesn't hold.

honest gaps

What we can't do yet

We cannot see whether a title actually transfers — that needs licensed data we haven't bought. The prediction model rests on 130 reappearances, which isn't enough. And every surface worth polling outside this platform blocks automated access.

the open question

Where would you spend it?

The 1,692 unhandled sellers, the pipeline forecast, or the history layer — one of these is probably worth real money to a dealer and the other two probably aren't. We don't know which. You do.
